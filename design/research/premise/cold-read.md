# Cold read: the front door (prototype v3)

Tested at 390 × 844 in both themes, plus the app at 1440. I wrote sections 1 to 5 before reading any brief.

## 1. What I believed

**After three screens:** "A cute inbox sorter. Little characters file your stuff into piles, and you can snooze things." The name and the flick-down scene point the same way.

**After the intro:** "An organiser you install with Cloudflare and some Anthropic key. It reads your mail and sorts it into folders."

**When I saw it was an AI that does the work:** only on Today, at "Can Dave see the loft on Tuesday at 10?", which already had a reply drafted. The intro never says AI, agent, draft or do.

## 2. Screen by screen

- **"Life sends you a lot."** A slogan nobody is saying, over generic icons. It isn't my week.
- **"Keep and the crew sort it."** "Keep" reads as a verb. "Sort" sounds like filing, not work. The deal is charming, but the crew only receive cards and never act on them.
- **"Only what needs you."** The card is blank. Tapping where the card was drawn did nothing, and when it worked I couldn't tell what the tick meant I had agreed to.
- **"Not now? Flick it down." / "It comes back."** The best scene.
- **"Nothing leaves without your yes."** The hold feels good, but I'm approving a blank card, so nothing is at stake. If I hold too long, the "That was your yes" screen gets skipped.
- **"Your Cloudflare. Your Anthropic key. Not ours."** A jump from toy to infrastructure. My attention dropped here.
- **Set-up:** "K4Q 72R", "Spending is capped at $60" (spending on what?), "App password", "secret address in iCal format". Wren, Scout and Juno show up as names with no jobs. "Mail connected." comes with no payoff.
- **"Keep is reading."** Money ticks up while nothing visible is found.
- **"Keep found 4 Spaces."** This should be the magic moment, but it's four labels ("A birthday": whose?) with no evidence and no plan.
- **"Routines start paused."** It ends on jargon and a blank envelope.

The app then contradicts the intro: Taxes, which I flicked away, comes back; mail is "from Scout", not Wren.

## 3. What is lifeless, ranked

1. **Empty cards.** The whole metaphor is cards, and none has words. Every tap is a ritual with nothing in it.
2. **Nobody speaks.** The mascots are mute and the copy reads like headlines. In the app, Keep speaks in the first person ("I have pencilled 14:30"). The intro never does.
3. **I'm not in the story.** The app has Maya, Dave and Mum's 70th. The intro has icons.
4. **The verbs are about filing:** sort, keep, flick. Nobody drafts, chases or books anything.
5. **The payoff is held back.** The reveal is a list of labels, not "here's what I already did".
6. **Pacing.** Six scenes of metaphor, then seven chores with no reward.
7. **Visuals.** Every scene has the same layout and the same pale wash, and each face is just two dots. Sound wasn't tested (headless).

## 4. What it never tells

That it's AI and does the work. It never shows a finished job, says what each crew member is for, what I stop doing, how often it will interrupt me, what it costs a month, or why I should trust it with my mail.

## 5. What should survive

- Flick down, "It comes back", and the time chip.
- Hold to say yes, and "Only practice. Nothing was sent."
- "Nothing leaves without your yes."
- The horizon, and the progress pips that become the crew.
- Keep dealing out the crew.
- The approval screen in the app (exact words, "Goes 30 seconds after you hold").
- Keep's buried line: "Tell me once, out loud or typed. I file it in the right Space and put someone on it." It's the best pitch in the prototype.
- The cap shown up front, and Skip everywhere.

## 6. After reading the brief: the gap

Later is a team of AI agents in the user's own account. They draft, chase and reconcile, and come back only for a yes on exact words, inside a cap. The intro explains the *interface* instead. ui-direction's idea that "the interface keeps time" turned into a tour of where cards go, not what the agents do. The team, the work and the agency are all missing.

Three changes:

1. **Fill the intro cards with real work.** Use one believable life. Dave's email arrives, Wren drafts the reply on screen, and the user holds to send *that* draft on the real approval layout. The snooze card says "Venue deposit, back at 6 pm".
2. **Let Keep speak, and give each crew member a verb.** Keep talks in the first person from the second screen. Introduce the crew by what they do: "Wren answers mail", "Scout chases people", "Ledger checks money". Say once, plainly, that they are AI.
3. **Make every set-up step pay back.** After mail, Wren names two real finds. "Keep is reading" streams finds, not dollars. Each Space arrives with its evidence and a first job already drafted. Put the infrastructure in human terms: "Runs in your own account. You pay the AI directly, capped at $60 a month."
