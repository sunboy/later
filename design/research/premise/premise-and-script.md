# Later: premise and first-minute script

**What is wrong now.** Six lines, no "I", nothing about the person. They rehearse gestures on blank cards. Nothing of theirs is ever on screen.

## 1. The premise

| | A. Later, kept | B. A staff you sign off | C. Only what needs you |
|---|---|---|---|
| Said to a friend | "You tell it 'later' and it actually comes back, with the legwork done." | "It's a small staff for my life admin, and nothing goes out without my yes." | "It reads everything, so I only see the four things that need me." |
| Human truth | "I'll do it later" is a small lie. The cost is carrying the open loop. | People want help but will not hand over the keys. | The volume is the problem. |
| For | Whoever runs a household's admin on top of a job. | People wary of AI agents. | The inbox-drowned. |
| Risk | Sounds like a snooze button, or promises reliability an AI cannot give. | Leads with fear. Describes the machine. | Every triage app says it. It is the current intro. |

**Choose A.** It is already the name. It is the only one about the person, and the only one they can test in the first minute by handing over a real thing. B becomes the terms, C the proof.

**Must not claim**
- "Done for you", "handled", "never forget". Say legwork: found, drafted, chased, ready for your yes.
- That it is right, or acts alone. Keep admits mistakes; nothing is sent, paid or deleted without a hold.
- "Private". Say exactly: your Cloudflare account, your Anthropic key, no Later server. The model on your key reads your mail.
- Free. It spends your key, up to your cap.

## 2. Who is speaking

Keep, in its own words:

1. **Plain.** Words you would use to a neighbour.
2. **Exact.** Names, times and amounts before adjectives.
3. **Brief.** One sentence, then I stop.
4. **Owning.** If I guessed, I say so. If I got it wrong, I say that first.
5. **Dry.** Calm, a little wry. I never cheer or nag.

I would never say: "I've got this!", "Don't worry", "As an AI", or "all done" when it is not.

| Situation | Lines |
|---|---|
| Greets | "I'm Keep. I hold on to things until they're due." / "Afternoon, Sam. One thing needs you. The rest can wait." |
| Asks | "What have you been putting off?" / "Dave Ellis or Dave Marsh? I have both." |
| Does not know | "I don't know when the deposit is due. The email doesn't say. Shall I ask the venue?" / "I guessed this belongs to House move. Move it if I'm wrong." |
| Reports | "Scout chased Owen twice. He says Friday." / "Filed 14 receipts. Two I couldn't read. They're in Taxes." |
| Says no | "I won't send that without you. It's ready when you are." / "That would go over this month's cap, so I've stopped." |

The crew:

- **Wren** (plans), dates and people: "Seventy invited, forty-one yes. Shall I nudge the rest?" / "I've pencilled Sunday. Say if that's wrong."
- **Scout** (chases), field notes: "Dave's replied. Tuesday at 10." / "Nothing from Owen. Trying again at 3."
- **Juno** (compares), options with a pick: "Three flights. The middle one is £132 less." / "I found four. Two aren't worth your time."
- **Ledger** (fees), figures first: "£240.00 to the venue. Not paid. That one is yours." / "October is out by £12.40. One receipt is missing."

## 4. Script B, worked

**The question** (beat 1)
1. What have you been putting off? ✔
2. What do you keep meaning to do?
3. What's been sitting on your mind?
4. Give me one thing you said you'd do later.
5. What can I take off you?

**The premise** (beat 6)
1. It comes back with the legwork done. Later, and I mean it. ✔
2. Say later. I'll bring it back.
3. You said later. I'll make it true.
4. Later, and this time it happens.
5. I don't forget. That's the job.

Option 5 overclaims.

**The terms** (beat 7)
1. I'll get things wrong sometimes. So nothing is sent, paid or deleted without your yes. ✔
2. I make mistakes. You get the last word.
3. I draft. You decide.
4. Nothing goes out until you hold to say yes.
5. I prepare. Only you can send.

**The doors**
- **Set me up.** "You'll need a computer, a Cloudflare account and an Anthropic key. The key is billed to you, up to a cap you set."
- **I'm already set up.** "On a paired device, open Settings, then Add a phone."

**The hard asks**
- Computer: "This part needs a computer. Open later.sh/go there and type this code."
- Mail: "Let me read your mail, so I can find what's open and draft replies. The model on your own key reads it, and nothing is sent or deleted without your yes."
- Reading pass: "I'll read your recent mail, newest first, to find what you've been carrying. It stops at {cap} of your key, and you can watch the meter."

## 5. Name and tagline

Alone, the wordmark reads as a snooze. With a line that makes it a promise, it works.

1. Say later. It comes back ready. ✔
2. Put it off properly.
3. Later, and I mean it.
4. For everything you said you'd do later.
5. Hand it over. Get it back ready.

Pick 1: the product in six words, and "ready" is honest (ready for your yes, not done).
