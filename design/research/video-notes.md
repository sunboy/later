# Notes from the two videos the founder shared (14 Nov 2026)

Watched in full: audio transcribed locally, frames reviewed as timestamped contact sheets. Revenue figures quoted in the videos are the presenter's claims and are not verified here. The videos promote the presenter's own tools; the design points stand on their own.

## Video 1: "Every million-dollar app has a genre" (9 min)

Claim: apps built by AI without direction look cheap because they are the average of every app (a gradient from calm apps, emoji from cute ones, stat cards from dashboards, white space from minimal ones). The fix is to pick a genre and follow its rules.

| Genre | For | Examples named | Rules | How to stand out |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quiet | productivity, planners, finance, travel, tools | Structured, Flighty | neutral ground, one accent, type does the work, big numbers, small labels, space | one signature moment worth a screenshot (Flighty's live flight, Structured's day as one timeline) |
| Character | wellness, habits, learning, companions | Duolingo, Finch, Tolan | a character with real emotions, rounded shapes, owned bold colours, big celebrations | give the character a job and a story; "a mascot sitting in the corner is decoration; a character that needs you is a product; if you deleted it, the app should stop working" |
| Atmosphere | sleep, meditation, focus | Calm, Opal | full-screen imagery or 3D, slow motion, little text, sound as design | one material nobody else owns, used everywhere (Opal's gem) |
| Data | fitness, health, money tracking | Bevel | one hero number per screen, colour means something, rings and gauges, numbers that roll | say what the number means in plain words |
| Loud | social, events, creators | Partiful | huge type, user content as the design, themes people pick, copy with personality; strict layout underneath | let people make it theirs |

Where it is going: apps you barely open (Widgetsmith, Locket, one sec). "The widget is the product; the app is the settings." Design the widget first, the notification second, the app last. Good notifications are specific, well-timed and human (Opal: "You've been on Instagram for 15 minutes. Let's cool down."; Duolingo: "These reminders don't seem to be working. We'll stop sending them for now.").

Rules premium apps share across genres: one job per screen; one system (one font family, one icon set, one spacing scale); motion that settles like real objects; copy that sounds like a person; design the moments AI skips: the empty screen, the error, the win (Opal's block screen: "You set this rule yesterday. Past you was right.").

Workflow: find the top earners in the category; pull their real screens; name the genre and extract its rules; find what all of them do the same way (the gap); add your colour, character and voice; write it into one design file every screen reads first.

## Video 2: Tolan teardown and "Ollie" (8 min)

Tolan is an AI friend with a character. Three design decisions named:
1. You do not sign up; you get a reading. A glowing crystal interviews you ("What brings out your best qualities?"), hands you a card about yourself, then matches you with a character. It is about you.
2. The ask comes at peak anticipation: after the reading, the match and naming it, just before you meet it.
3. It is a place, not a chat window: the character lives somewhere, you dress it, and before you say hello it asks permission to text you, so the friend reaches out first.

"Character, a world, and a friend who texts you first." The model is the same one everyone has; the character is the difference.

The presenter's own app, Ollie, for people with ADHD: an octopus, "because most of an octopus's brain is in its arms: eight arms, all going at once. Ollie juggles your eight things so you only have to hold one." Screens shown: "Meet Ollie." with one line of what it does; "Tell Ollie everything. Messy is fine."; one next thing handed back with a timer and the character staying with you; "What I remember about you", an editable notebook. Method: read the competitor's reviews for the repeated complaint (it forgets), pick one audience, build the character sheet (turnaround, expressions, poses, three animations: hello, working, celebration).

Growth notes: ads sell the moment you need someone, not features; one audience at a time; the character becomes the content people share.

## What this means for Later

1. Later is in the Character genre with a Quiet base. Keep must have a job and a story, visible everywhere: what Keep is holding is the state of the app. Test: if Keep were deleted, would the app stop making sense? Today it would not; Keep is close to a mascot in the corner.
2. The metaphor has to be sayable in one line, like Ollie's. Candidate: "Keep holds everything you've said 'later' to, so you don't have to."
3. Onboarding should introduce the product plainly, one idea per screen (founder: an opening like "An app that does things for you", then Spaces, the mascot, the agents, templates, cards), before asking the user to do anything.
4. Later is an "app you barely open": agents work while you are away. The lock-screen widget and the notification deserve design before more app screens. Notification copy should be in Keep's voice, specific and well-timed.
5. Design the skipped moments: empty Today, an agent's error, the cleared deck, a kept promise ("You said later. Here it is.").
6. A "what Keep remembers about you" notebook, editable, already fits the memory design.
7. One signature moment worth a screenshot: the day's deck dealt by Keep, or a card rising back from Later on time.
