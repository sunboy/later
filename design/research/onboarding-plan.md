# Onboarding plan: arriving at the Keep

14 Nov 2026. Evidence is in `feature-tour-research.md`. Keep's voice follows `premise/premise-and-script.md`.

**In short.** Explain before asking. The user arrives in a place and walks it with Keep, meeting one feature per stop as the real interface sitting in the world. Nothing is typed or required until the tour ends.

## 1. Three worlds

| | A. **The Keep** (harbour) | B. **The Sorting House** | C. **The Day** |
|---|---|---|---|
| In one sentence | "A small paper harbour where your things are kept safe and come back on time." | "A post room where your mail is opened, filed and answered." | "One landscape from dawn to night: now above the horizon, later below." |
| Explains the product? | Yes. Things go out on the evening tide and come back at dawn, and the harbour gate shows that nothing leaves without you | Filing, yes. Later and legwork, no | Later, yes. Spaces, crew and templates, no |
| Does every feature have a home? | Yes. Keep is the tower, the crew are boats and handcarts, Spaces are buildings on the quay, cards are letters, later is the tide, your yes is the gate, templates are plans on the noticeboard, your account is your bay | Mostly. Templates and later are forced | No. There is nowhere for the crew to live |
| Buildable, and trustworthy? | Cut paper is planes with thickness, and the characters are already cards. A harbour means shelter, so keep the water calm and free of peril | Sober, but busy and reads as drudgery | Easiest, but generic and abstract |
| Does it live on after onboarding? | Yes: the Spaces view, the Today header, the title screen, the widget, empty states and the evening | Only as a filing view | Only as a header |

**Choice: A, the Keep.** It is the only world where every feature is a place, and its central promise is a natural event there: the tide goes out at dusk and returns at dawn. It uses Keep's double meaning, the Harbour palette, the existing horizon and the card material. From B we keep one rule: the copy stays plain, and the world only shows.

## 2. Keep has a job the app cannot lose

**Keep's line:** "I'm Keep. I hold what you put off, so you don't have to."

Keep is the keeper and the tower is the keep, both made of cards. What Keep holds is the state of the app.

| User's state | World | App |
|---|---|---|
| Things handed over | The tower is one card thicker per item | Keep button fan |
| Crew at work | Boats out, each with a crew card | Next rows |
| Needs you | A clay light in a building window, and a letter at the top of the quay | Now deck, widget count |
| Later | One lamp per item along the wall, each with its time | Later dock |
| Evening | The sun sets, Keep sinks behind the wall, boats moor | Evening edition, "That's the lot" |
| Error or cap reached | Keep sits askew, or the lighthouse dims: "That would go over this month's cap, so I've stopped." | Error card, meter |

Delete Keep and nothing holds anything, so Keep passes the video's test.

## 3. Length: 8 stops, then the handover (about 45 seconds)

Strong openings show 1 to 4 beats before asking (YNAB, one sec, Opal, Cat On Chair), and Focus Friend holds 9 because every beat is story. Every feature must be met, so there are eight stops, one idea each: each plays by itself in 4 to 6 seconds, a swipe or tap moves on, Skip is always there, and progress shows as lamps lighting along the quay, not dots.

Crew and legwork share a stop, because the crew make sense only through what they do. Your account and the doors share one, because the account explains the setup.

## 4. The first five seconds

1. **0 s.** Dawn navy and the app's own horizon line; faint water (sound opt-in, speaker hint as in Tolan).
2. **0.6 s.** The camera sinks through paper cloud (Focus Friend) onto a cut-paper harbour on ink water: a tower at the end of a quay, four dark buildings.
3. **2 s.** The citrus sun touches the horizon; tower windows warm.
4. **2.8 s.** Keep, in the doorway, notices you: corner lifts, eyes turn to camera, one blink, a paper tick.
5. **3.5 to 5 s.** On a paper band: **"Later. It does what you put off."** The supporting line types on; the first lamp lights.

**Opening line options**

1. "An app that does things for you." The founder's line: plain, but any assistant could say it.
2. **"It does what you put off."** Under the wordmark: "Later. It does what you put off." Six words, about the person, and it explains the name. ✔ **Chosen.**
3. "Hand it over. It comes back ready." Better saved for stop 6.

**Supporting line:** "It's AI. It finds, drafts and chases, and asks before anything goes out." This is the only place the tour says it is AI.

## 5. The walk

The camera travels left to right along one continuous quay, with a 600 ms parallax pan between stops (slow sea, mid buildings, fast foreground posts). Dragging to look around and tapping a building to peek inside are optional. Each stop shows one real interface piece with one clearly-example item.

## 6. After the tour

1. **Try it (optional).** Keep asks "What have you been putting off?" with three chips; type or speak (script B). The card goes into the tower and out on a boat: "Back when I'm set up." There is no model before setup, and Keep says so.
2. **The doors.**
   - "Set up my harbour", with the line "About ten minutes on a computer. Your Cloudflare account and AI key, billed to you up to a cap."
   - "I already have Later", which pairs by QR.
   - "Look around first", which opens a labelled example Today.
3. **Setup.** No Later account or server; computer first, then the phone pairs by QR; the installer shows the same harbour. Every step explains rather than demands: a crew member walks onto the jetty carrying a preview of the card that connection produces, and one sentence covers need, consequence, refusal and undo.

| Step | Who | Shows | Says |
|---|---|---|---|
| Computer, then QR pairing | Keep | A laptop on the quay with a code, then a QR | "This part needs a computer. Open later.sh/go there and type this code." |
| Mail (app password) | Wren | A draft reply | "Let me read your mail to find what's open and draft replies. The AI on your own key reads it. Nothing is sent or deleted without your yes." |
| Calendar (private link) | Scout | A meeting brief | "Read-only. I see your times so I can chase around them." |
| Other apps | Juno | A school-trip card | "Apps that email you, like school or nursery, I read from your mail. No new logins." |
| First read (capped) | All | **The island builds itself from the user's life.** A building rises per Space, with evidence ("House move: 6 emails, completion 12 Dec"), next to the meter | "I'm reading your recent mail, newest first. I stop at £1." |
| First card | Keep | Dawn returns the user's tryout card | "You said later. Here it is." |

Cap amounts in this plan (£20 a month, £1 first read) are placeholders; real figures come from the user's settings and the price table.

Ask for notification permission only when the first card is due, showing a sample in Keep's voice (as Tolan and Pengu do).

**In the app:** Spaces becomes the aerial quay (light means needs you, a boat means crew out; swipe between harbours, as in Pokémon Sleep). Today gets a 120 px harbour band at the current hour (Headspace). The title screen is the arrival cut to 1.5 s. The empty state is "That's the lot." with boats moored and Keep reading. In the evening the tide carries out the day's lamps.

## 7. Widget and notifications

**Widgets:** lock-screen rectangle with the tower glyph and "2 need you · Scout back 15:00"; a circle with the tower and a count; home medium showing the harbour at the current hour with the next return. Offer them after the first card returns (Locket, Blessed): "What needs you finds you."

**Notifications**

1. "Dave replied: Tuesday at 10 works. Wren drafted a yes. It's waiting for you."
2. "You said later to the venue deposit. It's back, form found."
3. "Ledger: £240 to The Old Mill is due Friday. Not paid. That one's yours."
4. "These evening notes don't seem useful. I'll stop sending them."

## 8. Art direction

**Look.** A cut-paper diorama: every object is a card 2 to 4 px thick with a darker cut rim and a soft contact shadow. No outlines; no gradient without a light reason; paper grain is a static tile, never a live filter.

**Palette by time of day** (Harbour; reconcile with the tokens in `ui-direction.md`)

| Time | Sky | Water | Accents |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dawn | Cream to peach | Ink navy | Citrus sun |
| Day | Paper | Slate blue | Clay windows mean needs you |
| Dusk | Clay | Navy | Citrus lamps |
| Night | Ink navy | Black navy | Citrus lamps, clay windows |

**Dark mode** is the night harbour, not an inverted day.

**Type** never sits on scenery: one paper band in the lower third, scene in the top 60%.

**Motion:** transform and opacity only, at most three moving layers, one overshoot to settle, camera eased out (`cubic-bezier(.23,1,.32,1)`). **Reduced motion:** stills with cross-fades; Keep only blinks.

**3D routes, tested here**

| Route | Verified | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| CSS 3D cards for Keep and crew | Built Keep from three card planes with an edge, a gradient light that follows the rotation, and a contact shadow. A 24-frame turnaround was captured headless in 2.3 s | **Use, live.** It is distinctive, crisp, themeable and interactive (fan, flip, deal, fold) |
| Live three.js diorama | WebGL2 renders in the pre-installed headless Chromium on SwiftShader (software). A low-poly island with soft shadows ran 120 frames in 4.1 s, about 34 ms a frame on CPU. three.js r160 is 167 KB gzipped from cdnjs. Phone frame times are **unverified** | Not on phones in v1. Use it to author the plates; it may suit the desktop installer |
| Pre-rendered sprites and plates | A 24-frame sprite sheet at 2× is 59 KB as WebP (1.5 MB as PNG) | **Use** for fixed camera paths: 3 to 5 depth slices for parallax, plus water and boat loops. Live wins only where there is touch or theming |
| Generated illustration | Figma connector lists `generate_image`, `generate_vector_illustration` (not called) | Textures and studies only |
| Blender `bpy` | Not installed | Later: baked ambient occlusion and paper translucency on the plates |

**Live:** Keep, crew, cards, boom, lamps, text. **Pre-rendered:** island plates for four times of day, as layered WebP.

**Assets:** Keep rig (8 states); 4 crew rigs; island, tower, quay, gate, noticeboard and lighthouse plates at 4 times of day; 8 Space buildings (house, hall, desk, kiosk, school, clinic, garden, workshop), lit and unlit; boat and cart sprites; 12-frame water loop; cloud layer.

**Budgets:** tour ≤ 1.5 MB; first stop ≤ 400 KB and interactive in 1.5 s on 4G; each plate layer ≤ 120 KB at 2×; ≤ 4 ms main thread a frame; 60 fps on iPhone 12 or Pixel 6 class (to be measured).

**First proof:** one 390×844 scene. Three parallax plates of the dusk harbour, live CSS Keep in the doorway, one card sailing out and back at dawn; an 8-second loop in light and night, with a reduced-motion still and sound on and off.

## 9. What we are deliberately not doing

- No typing, questions or gestures in the tour, and no permission asks.
- No carousel dots, feature lists or icon grids.
- No live 3D world on phones in v1.
- No harbour words in buttons, setup or settings. The copy says card, Space, crew and approve.
- No peril near money or mail; no pretend finds before mail is connected; no dress-up, currencies or streaks.

## 10. Three biggest risks

1. **The metaphor leaks into copy** and a mail and money tool feels like a game. *Mitigation:* the world is visual only.
2. **The craft falls short** with no illustrator. *Mitigation:* founder signs off the first proof first; fallback is flat 2D layers.
3. **The spell breaks at the computer step.** *Mitigation:* time cost on the doors, the tryout card waiting, and the installer continuing the harbour.
