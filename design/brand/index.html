Later, drawn and moving

Five animated wordmarks drawn from our own letterforms, a small display face, a type study in the app’s real strings, and two ways to make Harbour light as strong as Harbour dark.

The recommendation is the word on a horizon: the r sinks to later and comes back on time. The engraved ball is for brand moments.

Drag the word down

Wordmark

Each one is a custom element of SVG or canvas and plain script, with an idle loop that is still most of the time, a working state, a done moment and a touch. Each is shown large, at 20px in the app header and beside Keep. Clay never appears in the wordmark; citrus appears only for done.

The ball

The founder's idea. LATER is engraved around a ball, inlaid in ink. The ball turns slowly to look one way, rests, then looks the other way, and the letters slide across the face and around the edge with real foreshortening.

Idle
Looks right, rests, looks left, rests, comes home: a 16 s loop that is still most of the time.
Working
Turns steadily. The word goes round the back and comes back, every 9 s.
Done
One full turn, then a citrus moment: the porcelain warms to citrus in light, the inlay turns citrus in dark.
Touch
Drag sideways to turn it by hand; it springs back. Tap for one turn. Hover and it leans toward you.

Use for brand moments: the splash, onboarding, the website, the About screen. In the header it becomes a slot across the equator of a larger ball so the word stays legible at 20px. Its working spin in a header would wear thin within a day.

Drag sideways, or tap

Late arrival

The letters settle one after another and the r is always a beat behind, as if it arrived late. The joke is quiet and the timing carries it.

Idle
Every 9 s the word nods from left to right. The r follows late and wobbles as it lands.
Working
A slow ripple runs through the word; the r lags behind it.
Done
For once the r is on time: all five land together and a citrus rule draws underneath.
Touch
Hover and the word leans in; the r catches up late. Tap and it arrives again, letter by letter.

The most charming at 20px and the cheapest to build. It is about lateness rather than time kept, which is close to the brand but not the same. The wobble would tire if it played every time the header appeared; once per session is right.

Hover, or tap

Below the lineRecommended

The word stands on a horizon. What can wait sinks below it and comes back when promised. In idle only the r sinks, so for a few seconds the logo reads Late, then the r returns on time.

Idle
Every 11 s the r sinks under the line, waits two seconds, and rises with one small overshoot.
Working
The letters ride a slow swell with their feet in the water.
Done
The word lifts clear and the horizon turns citrus and widens.
Touch
Drag the word down to push it to later; let go and it springs back. Tap and the whole word sinks and returns.

My pick for the app. It is the product's one idea in a single letter, it reads at 20px, it is still for nine seconds in eleven, and the drag is the app's own gesture. The least likely to tire: the motion is one letter, and the word always comes back whole.

The hand

The letters are cards held in one hand, like Keep. They fan a few degrees on a pivot far below the word and square up again. While moving, faint card faces show behind each letter.

Idle
Every 10 s the hand fans open a touch and squares up.
Working
The hand stays slightly open, card faces showing, and the cards riffle one after another.
Done
The hand squares up with a tap on the table and a citrus card slides up behind it, like Keep's done state.
Touch
Hover and the hand fans open; the card under your pointer lifts. Tap and the word is dealt again from the L.

The best partner for the mascot: the wordmark and Keep become one family. Strongest in the lockup and on first launch, where the deal plays once. The card faces in the working state read as tiles at header size; keep them for large sizes.

The t keeps time

The cross-bar of the t is a hand on a pivot at the stem. At rest it points forward, to three o'clock: later. The other letters carry a knockout edge so the hand can pass behind them.

Idle
Every 12 s the hand moves on one minute with a small mechanical tick, then eases home.
Working
The hand sweeps round, passing behind the a.
Done
One quick turn over a citrus face, landing home with one overshoot.
Touch
Hover and the hand points at you; leave and it returns to three o'clock.

The cleverest idea and the weakest logo: the word only reads while the hand is level, so it works as a one-off and as a loading state, not as a mark. A sweeping hand in a header reads as a spinner within minutes.

Pick: Below the line

It is the only one that says what the product does, things sink to Later and come back when promised, and it says it with one letter, so the word is whole and legible nearly all the time. At 20px the sinking r still reads. The drag is the gesture the app already uses for snoozing, so the logo teaches the product. Use the ball for the splash, onboarding and the website, where one slow turn is an event.

What would get tiresome

  • The t keeps time: a sweeping hand in a header reads as a spinner within minutes, and the word only reads while the hand is level.
  • The ball, working: a spinning ball in the header is the spinning-globe logo we wanted to avoid. Keep its working state for large sizes.
  • Late arrival: the r’s wobble is funny once a session and irritating every time a screen opens.
  • The hand, working: card faces behind each letter turn into tiles at small sizes.

The letterforms

The five letters are our own outlines, not a font set in text. They are drawn from centre lines with an oval pen, wider than it is tall, so stems come out heavier than bars. Every curve is a superellipse with exponent 2.75, the same squared-round corner as the app’s cards and Keep. The t’s bar points only forward, to the right, which is where later is on the line.

Choices

  • Squared rounds: the counters of a and e are card shapes, so the word and the deck are one material.
  • Forward-only t bar: the one asymmetry, and the clock hand in concept five.
  • Spacing by equal nearest distance between letters, measured on the outlines.
  • Lower case “Later” for the app; capitals “LATER” only on the ball, where wide, even letters wrap better around a sphere.
  • One heavier cut for the wordmark (bars 106, stems 127 units on a 690 cap) than for the display face (88 and 110).

Type

A full text typeface cannot be drawn well in one session, so the work is in three parts: the wordmark letters above, one small headline face as a test, and a study of real pairings from Google Fonts.

Skills

A search for font, typeface, type design, glyph and lettering skills found none. Only general design skills are installed.

Tools here

fontTools 4.68 is installed: it compiles TrueType, adds kerning from feature files, subsets, instances variable fonts and writes WOFF2 once brotli is added. shapely (outline geometry) and brotli install from PyPI; opentype.js is on npm. There is no FontForge or glyph editor, and Google Fonts sources are reachable for OFL work.

What is feasible

A display face drawn from code: yes, built below. Modifying an OFL face with fontTools (swap glyphs, rename, keep the licence): yes, not needed this time. A professional text face with hinting, many weights and full kerning: no, that is months of a type designer’s work.

Pairings in the app’s strings

A. Schibsted GroteskSchibsted Grotesk throughout

Character. A newspaper grotesk with real ink in the heavy weights: plain, quick and grown-up, one family to maintain.

Weakness. At 800 it shouts and the deck starts to look like a front page; nothing in it is specific to Later.

B. Familjen GroteskFamiljen Grotesk with Hanken Grotesk · recommended

Character. Familjen's narrow, slightly bookish shapes (the a, the g, the cut terminals) give headlines a calm, dry voice; Hanken is a quiet, open text face that never competes.

Weakness. Familjen has only four weights and no width axis, so the size-change motions have to carry hierarchy by size alone.

C. FrauncesFraunces with Albert Sans

Character. The most unhurried of the set: a soft old-style display that makes Today feel like a letter rather than a dashboard.

Weakness. Against Literata in the edition there are two serifs on one screen, and Fraunces at large sizes has become a common start-up look.

D. GabaritoGabarito with Wix Madefor Text

Character. Round, friendly geometric headlines over a very legible UI text face; the warmest of the sans options.

Weakness. Gabarito's round, toy-like bold slides towards the playful; beside money and sends it can look too cheerful.

E. UnboundedUnbounded with Instrument Sans

Character. Wide and loud: unmistakable at the top of the screen and very good for big numerals in the edition.

Weakness. Too wide for two-line decide headlines on a phone and too loud for an unhurried voice; it reads as crypto or a festival.

F. Mona SansMona Sans (current)

Character. Competent and neutral; the width axis is useful for motion.

Weakness. It is GitHub's face and looks like it; the founder does not like it and nothing in it says Later.

G. Later DisplayLater Display with Hanken Grotesk

Character. Our own squared-round headlines, the same curve as the card corners and the wordmark, so the brand and the screen are one material.

Weakness. A one-session draft: one weight, rough spacing and kerning, dark joins. Good for the wordmark and a few big words, not yet for every headline.

H. Young SerifYoung Serif with Funnel Sans (added)

Character. Added because the product's voice is unhurried and dry: a heavy, stubby serif reads like a well-made notebook, and Funnel Sans is crisp underneath.

Weakness. One weight only, and with Literata in the edition the screen has two serifs; it needs restraint to stay out of cafe-menu territory.

I. Funnel DisplayFunnel Display with Funnel Sans (added)

Character. Added as the one-family alternative to A: a 2024 superfamily whose display cut has small notches at the joins, a detail that holds at 20px and nobody else is using yet.

Weakness. The notches can look mannered in long headlines; newer and less proven than A or B.

Recommendation: B, Familjen Grotesk with Hanken Grotesk

Familjen has the dry, slightly old-fashioned grotesk shapes that suit an unhurried voice, it is narrow enough for two-line decide headlines at 22px, and it sits well beside Literata in the edition without being a second serif. Hanken is quiet and very readable at 13 to 15px. Runner-up: I, Funnel, if one family matters more than character. Keep Later Display for the wordmark and perhaps the single word Today.

Harbour light

Why dark is stronger. The surface steps are nearly the same in both themes (page to card is 1.24:1 in light and 1.21:1 in dark), so contrast numbers are not the problem. Three things are.

Depth

In dark, raised things are lighter, which the eye reads as nearer at once. In light, a white card on beige is a hue change plus a soft 16% shadow, so the deck looks printed on the page rather than lifted off it.

Clay on beige

Clay on navy is orange on blue, complementary and bright (7.4:1 for the heading). Clay on beige is warm on warm, so the needs-you colour goes brown and the Now heading loses its pull.

Tinted tiles

The draft and the Tomorrow button are beige tiles inside a white card (1.19:1). In dark the same tile reads as a recess; in light it reads as a stain. The screen ends up with only two dark anchors, the main button and the dock.

Which

Chalk is the safer change: only tokens move and every screen improves. Harbour wall is the stronger idea, because light and dark then share the same navy top, so the brand looks the same in both; it needs layout work on screens without a deck. Both keep the three colour jobs: clay for needs you, navy for the crew and Later, citrus for done or back.
