# Task: invent the experience of a personal AI agent app

You are proposing an original product and design CONCEPT. No code. Rough is fine. Words, a storyboard and simple text wireframes (ASCII or markdown boxes) are the deliverable.

## The premise (the founder's own words)

"It is a personal AI agent like any other AI agent out there. In general, they all boil down to some sort of AI agent orchestration layer in the backend, giving agents all the necessary tools they need to complete tasks, and with the computer it might have access to. That's all personal AI agents these days really are. And definitely with connectors for apps too, like Gmail. The premise is very simple, but the differentiation that we're bringing here is the organisation and the UI/UX layer, which all other chat-based AI agents are not offering at this point."

"It's like a very visual AI agent, which means that the UI should not be static at all times, but sort of a generative UI feel in which components grow, components shrink in size, or new components arise based on the context."

The agents he is comparing against are all chat-first: you type into a box and read a transcript. Examples: Meta Muse, Claude Duet, OpenAI Dots, Grok Bot, Instinct, Town, OpenClaw, Fo by Wajo.

Assume the backend exists and works: agents can read and act on mail, calendar, documents and the web, can run for a long time, and can remember. Your job is everything the person sees and touches.

## What is fixed

- It is for an individual's own life and work, on a phone first.
- Nothing is ever sent, paid for or deleted without the person's explicit yes.
- Agents may describe what to show only as data chosen from a set of components the app defines; the app draws the screen.
- The working name is "Later". You may use it, play on it, or ignore it.

## What is open: everything else

There is no existing design you need to respect. Do not assume any particular way of organising things, any mascot or character, any card, feed, inbox, board, timeline or chat metaphor, or any colour scheme. Invent the organising idea. If your concept has a character, it is because your idea needs one. If it has none, that is fine.

Fifteen other people are answering this same brief. The obvious answers (a chat with nicer bubbles, a to-do list run by AI, an inbox of cards, a dashboard of widgets) will be submitted by several of them and will lose to each other. Yours should have a central idea that the others are unlikely to have, and you should be able to say in one sentence what that idea is.

## What to deliver (one document, under 1,500 words)

1. **The central idea in one sentence**, then three sentences on how a person would describe the app to a friend.
2. **The organising model**: the few things a person has to learn, each in one line, and how they relate. Say what real-world thing or familiar behaviour the model borrows from, if any.
3. **A day with it**: morning, midday, evening, in six to eight lines, showing what the person sees and does and what the agents did while they were away.
4. **The core screens**: four to six, each with its one job and a text wireframe. Show what a person can act on, and show at least three specific places where the interface changes with context (what grows, shrinks, appears or leaves, and what causes it).
5. **Onboarding storyboard**: screen by screen from first open to the first useful moment. It must say plainly what the app is before asking the person to do anything, then make each thing in your organising model familiar, one at a time, using simple colour, graphics and motion. For each screen: the headline and one supporting line exactly as written, what is shown, what moves, and what the person does, if anything. Give a text wireframe for the first screen and two others.
6. **Look and feel in a short paragraph**: how colour, type and motion carry meaning in your concept.
7. **What makes it different from a chat agent, and its biggest risk**: two lines each.

Plain words. No hype. No invented user numbers, quotes or testimonials. Do not write code or HTML.

## How it will be judged

In this order:
1. **Originality of the central idea.** Would the other fifteen have thought of it? Does it give the product a shape no chat agent has?
2. **Desire.** Would a busy person want to open this, and want to act on what they see?
3. **Clarity.** Could a stranger explain it back after the onboarding?
4. **Coherence.** Does every screen follow from the one idea?
5. **Buildability on a phone**, with ordinary interface craft.

A safe, complete, conventional answer should lose to a distinctive one that is clear and usable.
